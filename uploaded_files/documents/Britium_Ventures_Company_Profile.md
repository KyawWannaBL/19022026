## Flexitanks for Liquid Logistics

Engineered for maximum efficiency in bulk liquid transport, our advanced flexitank solutions represent a paradigm shift for industries dealing with non-hazardous liquids. They offer a cost- effective, safe, and flexible alternative to traditional tankers and drums.

## What is a Flexitank?

A flexitank is a large, flexible bladder-like container that is fitted inside a standard 20-foot shipping container. Once filled, it transforms the container into a safe and efficient bulk liquid transport unit. After the liquid is discharged, the flexitank is removed and can be recycled, allowing the container to be used for standard dry cargo. Our Flexitank solutions are ISO 9001 Certified and FDA Compliant, guaranteeing the highest levels of safety and quality for sensitive cargo.

## Air Cargo Guide

## Calculating Chargeable Weight

In air cargo, space is as important as weight. Airlines charge based on Chargeable Weight, which is the greater of the Actual Weight or the Volumetric (Dimensional) Weight.

Volumetric Weight Formula: (Length cm × Width cm × Height cm) / 6000

Note: Some express carriers may use a divisor of 5000. Britium Express will always confirm the correct factor for your shipment.

## Example Calculation:

Package Dimensions: 60cm x 40cm x 40cm

Actual Weight: 10 kg

Volumetric Weight: (60 x 40 x 40) / 6000 = 16 kg

<!-- Page: 21 -->

Chargeable Weight: 16 kg (since it's higher than the actual weight)

## Packaging Guidelines for Air Cargo

Proper packaging is crucial for safe and cost-effective shipping.

1. Select the Right Outer Packaging: Choose a new, high-quality corrugated cardboard box. For heavy or fragile goods, a double-walled box or a wooden crate is recommended.

2. Use Proper Cushioning: Fill all empty spaces with materials like bubble wrap, air pillows, or foam sheets to prevent items from shifting.

3. Seal Your Shipment Securely: Use strong, pressure-sensitive packing tape (at least 2 inches wide). Apply the "H-taping" method along all seams.

4. Label Clearly and Correctly: Ensure all labels (Shipper, Consignee, AWB) are visible and securely attached. Use handling labels like "FRAGILE" or "THIS SIDE UP" if applicable.

5. Special Packaging by Cargo Type: Use triple-sealing for liquids, anti-static bags for electronics, and airtight packaging for food.

6. Secure Multiple Boxes: If shipping multiple boxes, securely bundle them together using straps or by shrink-wrapping them to a pallet.

## Incoterms & Pricing

## Understanding Incoterms

Incoterms are internationally recognized rules that define who is responsible for costs, risks, and tasks at each stage of an international shipment, preventing confusion and disputes.

<!-- [An infographic explaining common Incoterms.] -->

<!-- Page: 23 -->

## Common Incoterms Explained:

EXW (Ex Works): Seller makes goods available at their warehouse. Buyer is responsible for almost all costs and risks from that point forward.

FOB (Free On Board): Seller delivers goods on board the vessel at the port of shipment. Seller's responsibility ends once the goods are on the ship.

CIF (Cost, Insurance & Freight): Seller pays for costs and freight to bring the goods to the named port of destination and also procures marine insurance against the buyer's risk.
