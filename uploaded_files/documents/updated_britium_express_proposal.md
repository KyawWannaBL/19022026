## 2. The Dual Pricing Mechanism: Fairness and Transparency

At Britium Express, we utilize a dual pricing mechanism to ensure that you are billed fairly, regardless of your cargo's characteristics. A truck's capacity is limited by two factors: its maximum weight allowance (in Kilograms) and its available space (in Cubic Meters, CBM). Our pricing model accounts for both.

• Heavy, Dense Cargo (e.g., machinery parts, metal ingots) primarily consumes the truck's weight capacity.

• Light, Bulky Cargo (e.g., foam products, empty containers, furniture) primarily consumes the truck's space.

Our system calculates a price based on both weight and volume, and your final bill is determined by the factor that places the greater constraint on our vehicle's capacity. This ensures a fair price for every shipment. Below, we detail the two calculation methods.

## 3. Detailed Pricing Models

Our pricing is structured in tiers to offer more competitive rates for larger shipments. A minimum chargeable weight of 1,000 kg is required per round trip to ensure operational viability. If your shipment is less than 1,000 kg, it will be billed as if it were 1,000 kg.

## 3.2 Method B: Volume-Based Calculation (CBM Model)

This model addresses bulky cargo using a concept called "Chargeable Volume." The CBM rate is tiered to correspond with our weight-based pricing, using a standard conversion factor of 1 CBM = 250 kg.

Chargeable Volume Rule: The Chargeable CBM is the greater of the actual volume or the volumetric weight (Actual kg ÷ 250).

Formula for Chargeable CBM:

Chargeable CBM = MAX(Actual CBM, Actual kg ÷ 250)

The rate is then determined by the tier the Chargeable CBM falls into.

| Chargeable CBM Range | Equivalent Weight Range | Rate per CBM (MMK) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 - 4.0 CBM | 0 - 1,000 kg | 325,000 MMK |
| 4.01 - 6.0 CBM | 1,001 - 1,500 kg | 275,000 MMK |
| 6.01 - 8.0 CBM | 1,501 - 2,000 kg | 225,000 MMK |

## 4. Final Billing Rule and Comprehensive Example

To provide complete transparency, the final invoice amount is determined by a simple, overarching rule: you will be charged the higher amount resulting from the two calculation methods. This ensures that the charge accurately reflects the resources—either weight capacity or volume capacity—consumed by your shipment.

## Final Billing Rule:

```
Comprehensive Billing Walkthrough:
Let's consider a sample shipment with the following characteristics:

Actual Weight: 1,100 kg

Actual Volume: 7 CBM

Step 1: Calculate the charge using the KG Model.

The weight (1,100 kg) falls into the 1,001-1,500 kg tier.

KG-Based Charge = 1,100 kg × 1,100 MMK/kg = 1,210,000 MMK

Step 2: Calculate the charge using the CBM Model.

First, determine the Chargeable CBM:

Chargeable CBM = MAX(7 CBM, 1,100 kg ÷ 250) = MAX(7, 4.4) = 7 CBM

This Chargeable CBM (7) falls into the 6.01-8 CBM tier.

CBM-Based Charge = 7 CBM × 225,000 MMK/CBM = 1,575,000 MMK

Step 3: Determine the Final Invoice Amount.

Compare the results from Step 1 and Step 2:

Final Invoice = MAX(1,210,000 MMK, 1,575,000 MMK)
```

## 9. Fuel Surcharge Mechanism

To protect against volatile fuel price fluctuations while maintaining stable pricing for our customers, we implement a fuel surcharge mechanism. This mechanism activates when the average monthly fuel price exceeds or falls below predefined thresholds relative to our baseline of 2,500 MMK per liter.

The fuel surcharge is calculated using the formula:

Fuel Surcharge (%) = (Current Average Price - Baseline Price) / Baseline Price × 100 × Adjustment Factor

Where the adjustment factor is calibrated to reflect the proportional impact of fuel costs on our total operational expenses. The surcharge is applied to the base freight charges and communicated to customers at least 7 days before implementation.

This mechanism ensures that our pricing remains fair and transparent while protecting both our operational viability and your budget predictability.
